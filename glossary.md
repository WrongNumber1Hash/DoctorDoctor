## Possible Causes of Stomach Pain
Stomach pain is a very broad symptom that can indicate dozens of different illnesses, ranging from mild digestive upset to serious, life-threatening emergencies. Because the abdomen houses many vital organs, pinpointing the cause requires looking at the pain's exact location, severity, and accompanying symptoms.
### Common & Mild Causes
These are frequently caused by diet, infections, or temporary inflammation:

Indigestion: Discomfort, burning, or bloating in the upper abdomen often triggered by overeating or stress.

Stomach Flu (Viral Gastroenteritis) or Food Poisoning: Often accompanied by nausea, vomiting, or diarrhea.

Constipation or Gas: Can cause crampy abdominal discomfort, fullness, and bloating.

Food Intolerances: Conditions like Lactose Intolerance or Celiac Disease can cause abdominal pain, gas, and bloating after eating.

Urinary Tract Infection (UTI): Infections of the urinary tract can sometimes cause lower abdominal discomfort.

### Chronic Gastrointestinal Conditions

These are recurring or long-term illnesses that affect the digestive system:

Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS) or Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD): Chronic conditions like Crohn's disease or ulcerative colitis that cause recurring abdominal pain, cramping, and bowel changes.

Gastritis & Peptic Ulcer Disease: Inflammation or open sores in the stomach lining, which often cause sharp, burning stomach pain.

Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD): Chronic acid reflux that can cause a burning sensation in the upper abdomen and chest.

Gallstones & Cholecystitis: Stones in the gallbladder or inflammation of the organ, which typically cause sharp pain in the upper right abdomen, often after eating fatty meals.

Diverticulitis: Inflammation or infection of small pouches in the colon, leading to abdominal pain and changes in bowel habits.

Gynecological Causes (in females)In people with female reproductive organs, pain can be linked to:

Endometriosis: An inflammatory condition causing pelvic and abdominal pain, especially around menstruation.

Ovarian Cysts & Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID): Fluid-filled sacs or infections that cause lower abdominal and pelvic pain.

Ectopic Pregnancy: A medical emergency where a fertilized egg implants outside the uterus, causing severe lower abdominal pain on one side.

When to Seek Emergency Medical Attention

Severe, sudden, or worsening abdominal pain can indicate an emergency. Seek immediate medical care if your stomach pain is accompanied by:

Fever, chills, or shaking

Bloody or black, tarry stools

Persistent vomiting or vomiting blood

A rupturing appendix (Appendicitis) or a bowel obstructionInability to stand up straight, chest pressure, or shortness of breath

Here is the breakdown of abdominal conditions grouped by severity from 1 (mild) to 3 (emergency medical attention required):
## Severity 1: Mild & Manageable
These conditions are usually temporary, easily managed at home, or treated with over-the-counter medications.

* Indigestion: Temporary upper belly burning.
* Gas & Constipation: Cramping from trapped air or stool.
* Food Intolerances: Lactose Intolerance causing bloating after eating.
* Mild Stomach Flu: Viral upset causing brief nausea or diarrhea.

## Severity 2: Moderate & Chronic
These conditions require a formal doctor's diagnosis, targeted prescription medications, or long-term lifestyle management.

* IBS & IBD: Chronic illnesses like Crohn's causing recurring cramps.
* Gastritis & Peptic Ulcers: Sores in the stomach lining causing burning.
* GERD: Chronic acid reflux causing upper abdominal discomfort.
* Gallstones: Upper right side pain, often after fatty meals.
* Diverticulitis: Inflamed colon pouches causing localized pain.
* Urinary Tract Infection (UTI): Bacterial infection causing lower belly pressure.
* Endometriosis & Ovarian Cysts: Gynecological conditions causing pelvic pain.

## Severity 3: High Emergency
These are critical, acute conditions or complications that require immediate emergency medical evaluation or surgical intervention.

* Appendicitis: Severe, rapidly worsening pain in the lower right abdomen.
* Bowel Obstruction: A life-threatening blockage in the intestines.
* Ectopic Pregnancy: Severe one-sided lower abdominal pain during pregnancy.
* Peritonitis: Infection from a ruptured organ causing a rigid, extremely painful abdomen.

* Does the pain stay in one spot, or does it move around?
* Are you having any trouble keeping fluids down?

Here is the expanded breakdown of abdominal conditions reorganized into four distinct levels of severity, ranging from mild temporary issues to life-threatening emergencies.
## Severity 1: Mild & Self-Limiting
These conditions are temporary, harmless, and usually resolve on their own or with basic home care and over-the-counter remedies.

* Gas: Trapped air causing sharp, brief cramps or bloating.
* Constipation: Infrequent bowel movements causing a dull ache or fullness.
* Indigestion: Temporary burning or discomfort after eating a heavy meal.

## Severity 2: Moderate, Non-Emergency & Chronic
These conditions require a scheduled doctor's visit for a formal diagnosis, lifestyle changes, or daily prescription medications to manage.

* Food Intolerances: Celiac disease or lactose intolerance causing digestive distress.
* GERD: Chronic acid reflux causing frequent burning in the upper abdomen.
* Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS): A common disorder causing recurrent cramping and bloating.
* Mild Stomach Flu: Viral gastroenteritis causing temporary vomiting or diarrhea without severe dehydration.

## Severity 3: Severe & Requiring Prompt Medical Evaluation
These conditions are more serious, cause significant pain, and require timely medical care from a doctor or urgent care clinic to prevent worsening or major complications.

* Peptic Ulcers & Gastritis: Sores or severe inflammation in the stomach lining.
* Gallstones: Stones blocking bile ducts, causing severe upper right pain.
* Diverticulitis: Infected pouches in the colon causing localized lower left pain.
* Urinary Tract Infection (UTI): Bacterial infection causing lower belly pressure and painful urination.
* Endometriosis or Ovarian Cysts: Gynecological conditions causing intense pelvic pain.

## Severity 4: Critical Emergency (Seek Immediate Care)
These are acute, life-threatening conditions or severe complications that require immediate emergency room evaluation or emergency surgery.

* Appendicitis: A bursting or inflamed appendix causing severe lower right pain.
* Bowel Obstruction: A complete blockage in the intestines that stops food and fluids.
* Ectopic Pregnancy: A fertilized egg growing outside the uterus, risking severe internal bleeding.
* Peritonitis: An infection of the abdominal lining, often caused by a ruptured organ.
